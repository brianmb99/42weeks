# Whitsundays two-night family sea-kayak expedition options

## Purpose

This is a standalone, website-ready research brief for comparing three possible three-day/two-night family sea-kayaking expeditions in the Whitsundays. It deliberately does not assign calendar dates. The eventual itinerary should be selected after Salty Dog and Scamper review the family, boats, forecast, tides, campsite permits, and vessel schedule.

The family is comfortable with multi-night wilderness travel and substantial outdoor days. The remaining gating skill is specifically **loaded-tandem sea-kayak rescue and navigation**, because [Salty Dog requires prior kayaking experience, swimming ability, and proficiency in open-water rescue](https://www.saltydog.com.au/hire-and-rentals/).

## Dynamic ranking

| Rank | Route | Select it when | Approximate paddling | Camps |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| **1** | **Crayfish Beach → Maureen's Cove, Hook Island** | The forecast is genuinely calm and settled, with a favorable tide plan for the exposed northern/eastern Hook coastline | Approximately 8-12km for the camp-transfer leg, plus optional unloaded excursions | Crayfish and Maureen's Cove |
| **2** | **Whitehaven → Henning Island → Paddle Bay pickup** | Conditions support one serious 20-23km day and the Scamper tide schedule permits a Paddle Bay pickup | Approximately 29-32km total | Whitehaven and Henning, finishing on South Molle |
| **3** | **Whitehaven → Chance Bay → Henning pickup** | The family wants two more evenly divided transfer stages, more camp time, and less dependence on a final Paddle Bay pickup | Approximately 23-29km total, before optional Hill Inlet outing | Whitehaven and Chance, finishing on Henning |

### Is the reef-heavy route correctly ranked first only in very good weather?

**Yes.** When the northern Hook Island forecast is light, the reef-heavy route offers the best overall expedition:

- the two camps have substantially better shore snorkeling than Whitehaven or Henning;
- the transfer day naturally passes several of the Whitsundays' notable fringing-reef bays;
- it feels more remote and less resort-adjacent;
- the core camp-to-camp mileage is moderate, leaving time for snorkeling rather than treating the reef as scenery passed under time pressure.

It should not remain #1 in mediocre weather. The route involves exposed water around Hook Island's eastern and northern points, few dependable communications, mid-to-high-tide campsite landings, and less convenient bailout support. Maureen's Cove is sheltered from south-easterlies but exposed to strong northerlies. The decision must use the [Mackay Coastal Waters forecast](https://www.bom.gov.au/qld/forecasts/mackay-coast.shtml), the local inshore forecast and tides, and Salty Dog's route briefing—not the general Airlie Beach weather forecast.

For planning purposes, "really good/calm" means a forecast scenario around light winds—ideally roughly 10 knots or less—low swell, good visibility, no thunderstorm or squall risk, and no significant wind-against-tide problem around the exposed points. That is a preferred scenario, not a remotely prescribed safety limit. Salty Dog should set the actual go/no-go limits using the forecast, paddlers, boat models, and tide gates.

## Option 1: reef-heavy Hook Island expedition

### Route summary

**Scamper drop at Crayfish Beach → camp at Crayfish → paddle the northern Hook Island coast with reef stops → camp at Maureen's Cove → Scamper pickup from Maureen's Cove**

The point-to-point route is not a formally measured Ngaro Sea Trail stage. Crayfish and Maureen's Cove are only about 3km apart in a straight line, but the real route rounds the coast, enters bays, and deliberately adds snorkeling stops. Use **8-12km as a preliminary paddling range**, then replace it with the exact Salty Dog chart plot.

### First camp: Crayfish Beach

[Official Crayfish Beach page](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/whitsunday-islands/camping/crayfish-beach-hook-island)

- Location: Mackerel Bay South on Hook Island.
- Setting: sandy/dirt camp in dry rainforest, sheltered by a rocky eastern headland and mountains to the north and west.
- Capacity: maximum 12 people in one open camping area.
- Facilities: composting toilet only.
- Communications: no mobile reception.
- Landing: shallow craft at mid-to-high tide.
- Reef: extensive fringing reef flat only metres from camp; this is the strongest campsite-snorkeling proposition of the three itineraries.
- Operational caution: no anchoring within reef-protection markers; the closest public moorings are at Pinnacle Bay.

#### Arrival-day plan

1. Unload the two doubles, compact camp equipment, food, and water from Scamper.
2. Rig and pack both boats exactly as they will be paddled the next day.
3. Conduct a short loaded-boat shakedown and confirm that both rudders/skegs, spray decks, pumps, tow systems, deck lines, and child foot positions work.
4. Set camp above the relevant tide line and secure food from goannas and crows.
5. Snorkel the Crayfish reef around the locally recommended slack-water window. Queensland Parks says snorkeling and diving are generally best approximately one hour either side of high or low tide.
6. Inspect the launch route across the reef flat for the next morning's tide.

### Camp-transfer paddle: Crayfish to Maureen's Cove

This should be an early, deliberately unhurried reef day rather than a mileage day. The exact landing and snorkeling sequence must remain conditional on swell, current, day-boat traffic, tide, and the Salty Dog briefing.

#### Leg 1: Crayfish Beach to Mackerel/Pinnacle area

- Leave Crayfish with enough water over the reef flat and avoid dragging loaded boats across living coral.
- Follow the eastern Hook coastline toward Pinnacle Point.
- Use the Mackerel Bay/Pinnacle Bay area as the first regroup and weather-assessment point.
- Do not assume a sandy landing at the point. The chart briefing should identify the viable shore and the line around the reef.
- If the sea state at the point is materially worse than predicted, return to Crayfish rather than committing around northern Hook.

#### Leg 2: Pinnacle area to Manta Ray Bay

- Round the exposed point only in the planned tide/current window.
- Manta Ray Bay is a notable snorkeling area with a small beach and public moorings, but anchoring is prohibited in the no-anchoring area.
- Treat this as the principal mid-route snorkel stop if conditions and vessel traffic allow.
- Land or raft the kayaks only using the method approved in the local briefing; do not improvise an anchor on coral.
- Keep the stop bounded so the family is not exposed to strengthening afternoon sea breeze around the remaining northern coast.

Useful references:

- [Whitsundays public-moorings map](https://elibrary.gbrmpa.gov.au/jspui/retrieve/c44bbc38-d911-46b7-9f95-38c26a2b02af/2024-11-Public-Moorings-Map-Whitsundays.pdf)
- [Whitsundays Plan of Management](https://www2.gbrmpa.gov.au/access/locations/whitsundays-plan-management)

#### Leg 3: Manta Ray Bay to Butterfly Bay

- Continue west along the northern Hook coast.
- Butterfly Bay offers another possible reef stop, but it is popular with tour and charter boats.
- Maureen's Cove and Butterfly Bay are within Marine National Park/green-zone areas where fishing is prohibited. All fishing gear should remain stowed.
- Either snorkel briefly or use Butterfly as a protected rest/regroup point; there is no need to force a second long snorkel after Manta Ray.

#### Leg 4: Butterfly Bay to Maureen's Cove

- Finish at Maureen's Cove while sufficient water remains over the reef approach.
- The final approach is more protected from a south-easterly wind but not from a strong northerly.
- Land, unload, and carry boats above the tide before beginning camp setup.

### Second camp: Maureen's Cove

[Official Maureen's Cove page](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/whitsunday-islands/camping/maureens-cove-hook-island)

- Setting: shaded coral-rubble beach beneath Hook Island's rugged hills, with pandanus, beach gardenias, and a small creek behind camp.
- Capacity: maximum 24 people in one open area.
- Facilities: composting toilet and picnic tables.
- Communications: no mobile reception.
- Landing: mid-to-high tide.
- Exposure: sheltered from south-easterly winds; exposed to strong northerlies.
- Reef: fringing reef along the eastern and western sides of the cove.
- Fishing: prohibited in the green zone.

#### Final morning possibilities

The timing depends on Scamper's one-run, tide-driven pickup:

- snorkel the eastern or western side of Maureen's Cove around slack water;
- make a short unloaded coastal paddle back toward Butterfly Bay;
- paddle west toward the Steens coastline without landing in the closed area;
- remain at camp and use the morning as weather margin.

### Critical October correction: Steens Beach is unavailable

[The official Steens Beach page](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/whitsunday-islands/camping/steens-beach-hook-island) states that the campground is **seasonally closed from October 1 through March 31 for turtle nesting**. The trip is in October, so Steens cannot be the second camp or a landing/day-use stop. It can be viewed from the water only if the current park closure terms allow the route offshore.

This is why the October Hook itinerary is **Crayfish → Maureen's Cove**, not Crayfish → Steens.

### Option 1 bailout logic

1. **Before committing around Pinnacle Point:** return to Crayfish.
2. **After rounding the point:** use the sheltered bay selected in the Salty Dog briefing; do not assume Scamper can make an immediate pickup.
3. **If northerlies are stronger than expected:** Maureen's Cove may become a poor destination despite being protected from south-easterlies.
4. **If the entire northern coast is unsuitable before departure:** convert to two nights at Crayfish with unloaded local paddles, or switch to Option 2 or 3 before Scamper departure.

## Option 2: Whitehaven–Henning–Paddle three-island traverse

### Route summary

**Scamper drop at South Whitehaven → camp at Whitehaven → 20-23km southern Whitsunday paddle to Henning → camp at Henning → approximately 9km paddle to Paddle Bay → Scamper pickup**

This is the most satisfying broad Whitsundays expedition: two camps on different islands and a finish on a third. It ranks below Hook in a truly calm reef window because Whitehaven and Henning have weaker shore snorkeling. It ranks above Option 3 for variety and expedition shape.

The [official Whitsunday Ngaro Sea Trail map](https://parks.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0022/162652/wnst-map.pdf) gives approximately 23km from Whitehaven to Henning on the marked route and 9km from Henning to Paddle Bay.

### First camp: South Whitehaven

[Official Whitehaven camping page](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/whitsunday-islands/camping/whitehaven-beach-whitsunday-island)

- All-tide landing.
- Ten defined sites, maximum 36 people.
- Hybrid toilets and one communal sheltered picnic table.
- No drinking water or mobile reception.
- Good walking and iconic scenery; weaker snorkeling than Hook Island.
- South Whitehaven is not Hill Inlet. Hill Inlet is at the northern end of the beach.

Arrival-day activities:

- establish camp and perform a loaded-boat check;
- walk the 1.2km Solway circuit;
- explore South Whitehaven after the day boats leave;
- consider the 18-20km Hill Inlet/Tongue Bay return paddle only if Scamper lands early and the weather, tide, and daylight are unusually favorable.

### Main paddle: Whitehaven to Henning, approximately 20-23km

A 20km family paddle should include **at least two planned shore breaks**, plus contingency beaches identified on the supplied chart.

#### Stage 1: South Whitehaven through Solway Passage to Chance Bay

- Planning distance: approximately 8km.
- Time the Solway transit using Salty Dog's tide plan. Wind against current can make this a very different passage from the chart distance.
- Make Chance Bay the first planned landing if the tide and south-easterly exposure permit.

[Official Chance Bay page](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/whitsunday-islands/camping/chance-bay-whitsunday-island)

Chance provides:

- a real sandy landing and formal campground;
- composting toilet and picnic tables;
- shade, protected rest space, and a sensible meal stop;
- an alternate overnight campsite if the route must be shortened.

Chance is best approached around mid-to-high tide and can be difficult in south-easterly winds. Do not force this landing merely because it is on the itinerary.

#### Stage 2: Chance Bay to a southern Whitsunday cove

- Select a second rest beach such as Turtle Bay or another suitable southern cove during the Salty Dog chart briefing.
- The chosen beach should fit that day's wind direction and tide rather than being hard-coded now.
- Use this stop for food, water, child comfort, navigation check, and an honest decision about the Fitzalan/Henning crossing.
- If the group is running late or conditions have changed, remain at the agreed bailout site and contact the operators rather than pressing into a late crossing.

#### Stage 3: southern Whitsunday coast through the Fitzalan/Hamilton area to Henning

- Expect commercial, resort, and ferry traffic.
- Current patterns can be complicated; a visually calm surface is not sufficient route information.
- Maintain the two tandems as a tight group and cross traffic lanes decisively.
- Northern Spit on Henning is an all-tide landing and the functional reward at the end of the long day.

### Second camp: Northern Spit, Henning Island

[Official Henning Island page](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/whitsunday-islands/camping/northern-spit-henning-island)

- Open camp beneath casuarinas, maximum 18 people.
- Composting toilet and picnic tables.
- All-tide access.
- Mobile reception is generally available.
- Hamilton Island remains visible, making the setting less wild but communications and pickup more reliable.
- Exposure to winds and strong currents remains possible.

### Final paddle: Henning to Paddle Bay, approximately 9km

[Official Paddle Bay page](https://parks.qld.gov.au/parks/molle-islands/camping/paddle-bay)

- Cross northwest from Henning to South Molle using the current and traffic plan established in the briefing.
- Paddle Bay requires a mid-to-high-tide landing.
- If Scamper can collect late enough, use the spare time for a short South Molle walk.
- If the only collection window is too early, eliminate this leg and arrange pickup at Henning.

## Option 3: Whitehaven–Chance–Henning modular route

### Route summary

**Scamper drop at South Whitehaven → camp Whitehaven → approximately 8km paddle to Chance Bay → camp Chance → approximately 15km official-route paddle to Henning → Scamper pickup**

This is the most modular route:

- the transfer legs are more evenly divided;
- Chance is both a destination and an obvious bailout;
- there is more time for snorkeling, the Chance–Whitehaven walk, and child-led exploration;
- the final pickup is at an all-tide site with mobile reception.

The drawbacks are that both camps are on Whitsunday Island, Chance has no reception and difficult south-easterly access, and the route finishes rather than camps on the second island.

### First transfer stage: Whitehaven to Chance, approximately 8km

- Time Solway Passage correctly.
- Use protected coves along southern Whitsunday as optional micro-stops.
- Land at Chance around the useful tide level.
- If everyone wants additional activity after camp is established, use part of the Chance Bay walking track rather than relaunching loaded boats.

### Chance Bay camp

- Four defined sites, maximum 12 people.
- Composting toilet, picnic tables, and timber tent platforms.
- No mobile reception.
- Good snorkeling and frequent turtle sightings.
- Approximately 3.6km return walking connection toward Whitehaven/Solway.
- Good anchorage in northerlies but difficult access in south-easterlies.

### Second transfer stage: Chance to Henning, approximately 15-21km

The official route-planner figure is around 15km. Family expedition accounts have recorded approximately 21km when shoreline detours or a navigation error were added.

Plan two stops:

1. **Southern Whitsunday cove/Turtle Bay area:** early rest and condition check.
2. **Final pre-crossing beach selected by Salty Dog:** meal, water, traffic and current assessment before the Henning crossing.

Do not count Hamilton Island facilities as a casual bailout. Any resort landing, marina entry, or emergency arrangement should be discussed with the operators.

### Optional iconic addition

If Scamper delivers the family to Whitehaven early, an unloaded Whitehaven–Hill Inlet/Tongue Bay return paddle adds approximately 18-20km. Keep it optional until the actual arrival time and tide are known. Combining that outing with the two transfer legs produces an approximately 41-49km expedition.

## Equipment and load specification common to all options

### Kayaks

Use two **decked double sea kayaks**, not double sit-on-top recreational kayaks.

[Salty Dog kayak-hire page](https://www.saltydog.com.au/hire-and-rentals/)

The current hire includes:

- boat, paddles, PFDs, and spray skirts;
- spare paddle;
- dry bags;
- waterproof charts and tide tables;
- flares, V-sheet, whistle, pump, sponge, and vinegar.

Confirm:

- children's ages, weights, PFD sizes, and spray-deck fit;
- exact kayak model and total payload;
- hatch opening dimensions and usable volume;
- rudder/skeg configuration;
- whether both adult positions permit effective solo control if a child stops paddling;
- three-day pricing and damage deposit;
- tow system and handheld VHF availability.

### Camping load

Scamper's normal large camp kit and 55-litre cooler are inappropriate for a loaded point-to-point kayak trip. Pack compact backcountry equipment:

- two compact two-person tents;
- four compact sleeping mats and lightweight sleeping systems;
- one small tarp;
- one compact stove and minimal cookware;
- condensed/dehydrated meals and high-calorie snacks;
- small hard wildlife-resistant food containers that fit the hatches;
- full stinger suits, masks, fins, and reef shoes;
- minimal clothing and robust sun protection;
- repair kit, first aid, headlamps, knife, line, tape, and cable ties.

### Water

[Scamper recommends 5 litres per person per day](https://www.whitsundaycamping.com.au/equipment/). For four people over three days:

- minimum planning load: **60 litres/60kg**;
- nominal split: approximately 30 litres per tandem;
- add emergency reserve only after the exact boat payload and remaining gear weight are known.

Scamper can arrange a food or water drop, currently starting around A$50, if the drop aligns with its route. For three days, carrying the full supply is operationally simpler if the boats accept the load.

### Communications

- PLB carried on an adult's PFD, not inside a hatch.
- Satellite messenger or satellite phone.
- Waterproof Telstra phone in each boat.
- Handheld marine VHF, preferably one per boat or one primary plus redundant satellite communication.
- Paper/waterproof chart and compass in each boat.
- Route, overdue time, and emergency contacts filed with Salty Dog, Scamper, mainland accommodation, and a family contact.

## Website-builder guidance

Suggested page structure:

1. **Overview page:** dynamic ranking, weather-dependent decision rule, map, shared equipment, and operator roles.
2. **Option 1 detail page:** Crayfish and Maureen images, bay-by-bay route, weather gate, campsite facts, and bailout logic.
3. **Option 2 detail page:** three-island map, two planned stops on the 20-23km day, camp comparison, and pickup dependency.
4. **Option 3 detail page:** modular stages, Chance Bay detail, optional Hill Inlet addition, and why it is operationally simpler.
5. **Comparison page:** distance, reef quality, camp remoteness, communications, tide sensitivity, bailout quality, estimated cost, and "choose this when" filters.

The website should visually label every distance as one of:

- **official route-planner distance**;
- **operator/account-derived estimate**;
- **provisional planning range—confirm locally**.

Do not draw a precise Hook Island route line from this prose alone. The route map should remain schematic until Salty Dog supplies or approves waypoints.
